About me

Growing up in Cary, North Carolina, I was exposed to a wide variety of research and extracurricular activities that deepened my interest in medicine and music. My middle school STEM Research club sparked my involvement in research competitions, which I have competed in since 7th grade. Through competitions like NCSEF and NCSAS, I built my communication and presentation skills with judges and other presenters in various STEM categories.

These networking skills helped me attain a research opportunity with a toxicology professor in the Triangle Molecular Toxicology lab. This opportunity drew me into drug toxicology and dried blood spot testing. I learned a wide variety of analytical methods, including solvent extraction, LC/MS, and sonication, and I developed an effective dried blood spot extraction method for a commercial drug panel. This project pulled me further into learning lab techniques, so in the summer of my junior year, I reached out to the FPMV lab at UNC, which focuses on genetic quality control and endogenous retrovirus research across mouse strains. Through the FPMV lab, I developed skills in PCR, gel electrophoresis, primer assay design, and DNA extraction.

Through the Village Music School in Cary, NC, I developed my foundational piano and tonal theory skills. I later honed my interests into western classical music and studied works by Chopin and Rachmaninoff. I performed these works for audiences in recitals and retirement centers. I am interested in how emotional regulation and cognition are affected by specific classical music structures like tension-resolution, counterpoint, and uncertainty.

I now attend the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill and am majoring in Neuroscience and Music. Through these majors, I intend to further my interests in how music intersects with neuropsychology and medicine. I am currently working on a project mapping genetic determinants of germline endogenous retrovirus activity in CC015/Unc mice through the FPMV lab.
